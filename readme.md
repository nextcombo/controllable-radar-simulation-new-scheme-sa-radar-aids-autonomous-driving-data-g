Introduction With the rapid development of autonomous driving technology, radar, as an environmental perception sensor in advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), plays a crucial role. It possesses excellent anti-interference capabilities and performs stably under adverse weather conditions, well adapting to complex traffic environments.
However, despite its wide application in the automotive industry, the process of data acquisition for radar still faces challenges. Large-scale real scene data collection not only costs high but also carries certain risks, highlighting the importance of radar simulation technology. Simulation not only reproduces various environmental conditions and traffic scenarios but also helps researchers explore different radar attribute settings to address the diversity and complexity of real applications.
The existing radar simulation methods can be mainly divided into two categories: generative radar simulation and physical-based radar simulation. Generative radar simulation utilizes GAN or VAE to directly generate radar data in specific domains, generating data that is close to real data in simple 2D tasks. However, it faces issues such as large differences in data distribution across different radar systems and the lack of physical priors, which prevents extrapolation of radar attributes.
Physical-based radar simulation generates data by modeling the complete process from electromagnetic wave emission to reception. While it ensures interpretability and controllability of radar attributes, it requires precise radar hardware specifications, complex signal processing algorithms, and long computation times. Recent studies have used standard 3D reflection signals (single point reflection signals, also known as point spread functions, PSF) to characterize radar attributes, but each type of radar still requires separate measurements.
In light of this, this paper proposes the SA-Radar radar simulation method, capable of handling different radar attributes simultaneously, focusing on simulating the RAD tensor (i.e., the radar cube), and providing a complete workflow from environmental simulation to radar cube simulation. The radar cube simulation is realized through ICFAR-Net, which is based on 3D U-Net and incorporates radar attribute embeddings, using the waveform parameters of standard 3D reflection signals to represent radar attributes, making it intuitive and easy for models to capture signal changes under different attributes. Additionally, the study also proposes various methods to construct a rich dataset with radar attributes for robust training of ICFAR-Net.
Experiments not only validated the accuracy of SA-Radar in simulating different datasets but also tested it in downstream tasks such as semantic segmentation and 2D/3D detection. The results showed that the baseline models trained on simulated data could perform equally or even better than those trained on real data, and when combining simulated data with real data, all downstream tasks could further improve performance. Moreover, SA-Radar can achieve realistic simulations from new sensor perspectives and edited scenes, showing great potential in generating new scenarios for autonomous driving.
IV. Conclusion The paper proposes SA-Radar, a complete radar data simulation method that can simulate radar cubes under defined and controllable radar attributes for any scenario. By incorporating waveform parameter-based radar attribute embedding, SA-Radar can efficiently capture the changes in radar cubes under different radar attributes and accurately simulate radar data distributions that match real data distributions in terms of distance, azimuth, and Doppler dimensions.
Extensive experiments have shown that this method can significantly improve the performance of existing models in various downstream tasks, proving its potential to become a universal radar data generation engine in the autonomous driving field.
However, SA-Radar also has limitations. Although waveform parameter-based radar attribute representation makes attribute control simple and intuitive, it limits the perception of more detailed radar attributes, and cannot fully replace real data in tasks such as 3D target detection that require high precision in data distribution. Additionally, the lack of benchmarks makes it difficult to systematically and comprehensively compare this method with existing methods. Future research will focus on addressing these issues to promote the development of the radar simulation field.
